## What changed along the north bank

The water has fallen since September. A narrow strip of pale gravel now connects the footbridge to the bend, exposing roots that were submerged on our last visit.

Most of the change is small. It becomes visible when we stop at the same places and compare what we can actually see, rather than what we remember.

> Keep observations separate from explanations. A quieter morning does not, by itself, mean there are fewer birds.

At the willow grove, three fresh tracks cross the soft mud. Their edges are sharp, but we cannot tell whether they appeared before or after the overnight rain. Photograph them with a scale next time.

- The gravel bar is accessible from the path.
- Two fallen branches now catch leaves at the bend.
- The reeds near the footbridge remain green; those farther upstream are beginning to yellow.

The most useful comparison will be another visit at the same hour. Changing the route, time, and weather together would make the differences harder to interpret.

## Measurements and uncertainties

These measurements describe three stops on one walk. They are enough to choose where to look next, but too sparse to establish a seasonal trend.

The photographs need short captions before they become useful evidence. Include the direction of view, the stop name, and anything outside the frame that could explain a change.

### Before the next visit

1. Bring the longer pole and a small ruler for photographs.
2. Repeat the route from the footbridge, starting at the same time.
3. Compare the waterline before consulting last month's interpretation.

## Questions to carry forward

Does leaf accumulation at the bend persist after rain, or does the next rise in water clear it? Revisit this before describing the branches as a lasting obstruction.

The quietest part of the walk was the return through the willow grove. We heard traffic from the opposite bank and only one bird call. Record listening time on the next visit so that silence has a useful context.

For now, keep the explanation provisional. The next observation should be able to change it.
